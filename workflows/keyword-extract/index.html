    Keywords from archival text: statistical, and from the controlled vocabularies¶

    The keyword-extract workflow. Tool: atrium-keyword-extract. It takes over both kinds of keywords that used to be made in two places: the statistical keywords of nlp-enrich and the vocabulary-controlled keywords of llm-enrich. nlp-enrich keeps only the LINDAT calls.

    Scope

    This page gives the stable core of the workflow: its purpose, its steps, the formats it reads and writes, and the licence floor of its output. The methods' options, the models and the prompts are documented with the code — in the tool's README, whose documentation map leads to its usage, how-it-works and reference pages, and its service README — because they change as methods are compared and chosen. Both kinds now live in the tool: the statistical kind since its first release, the controlled kind since v1.1.0-beta. No controlled category has been checked against a reviewed answer yet, so treat the categories as research output.

    Purpose¶

    Finding an archival document by what it is about needs its keywords, and two kinds answer different questions. Statistical keywords are the phrases that characterise a document or a page against the rest of its text; they need no vocabulary and say how they were found. Controlled keywords are given per line: a language model picks the term of the AMČR keyword lists or the TEATER thesaurus that describes the line, and can only pick a term that exists in the vocabulary. With the term come the records behind it, the Czech keywords found in the line with their English translations, and the page and line it came from. The two kinds are kept apart in the output, and every statistical keyword says which method produced it.

    At a glance¶
    In the ATRIUM document record, after nlp-enrich; or plain text. Since v1.5.0-beta the statistical kind also reads files, on the command line (keywords.py) and as POST /extract_keywords's file alike: nlp-enrich's CoNLL-U or TEITOK, a line table (CSV) or a .txt. The controlled kind's batch clients read line tables (CSV), TEITOK or records
    Out statistical keywords with their method, score and rank, per document and per page; controlled keywords per line (JSON); the run's paradata (JSON). With a record sent: the record with its keywords block (statistical kind) and its enrichment block (controlled kind), side by side. The batch CLI writes CSV tables and, on request, each page's keywords (--per-page) and the record's keywords block (--document-json-out-dir), as the service does
    Runs as an HTTP service image (POST /extract_keywords, /extract_keywords_text), the production image; a command-line tool for the statistical kind (keywords.py) and its image; batch clients for the controlled kind; a research run on GPUs (llm_run.py) and its image
    Compute CPU for YAKE and the legacy method; a GPU is used by KeyBERT when there is one; the service's controlled kind calls a language-model service and carries no weights; the research run loads a model's weights onto GPUs
    Network the Hugging Face Hub for the KeyBERT model, on first use (the service fetches it at startup); for the controlled kind, the language-model service (OpenRouter, or a local Ollama). Only the vocabulary build reaches the AMČR and TEATER sources
    Code licence MIT
    Component licences KeyBERT (MIT) with its sentence-transformers model (Apache-2.0), YAKE (AGPL-3.0) when chosen, the AMČR and TEATER vocabularies (CC0), and for the controlled kind the language model's own terms; each run lists the ones it used. These do not by themselves set the rights in the output: Licences and paradata

    Steps¶

    # Step What happens In → out Activity
    1 Select the text The lines of the record are gathered per document and per page. Lines categorised Trash, Garbage, Inverted (a born-digital text layer that does not decode) or Empty are left out. Methods that need linguistic annotation use the lemmas and part-of-speech tags that nlp-enrich wrote. JSON → text —
    2 Statistical keywords KeyBERT (the default) ranks candidate phrases by their similarity to the document's embedding; YAKE ranks them without a model; the legacy method counts nouns, proper nouns and adjectives by lemma. Scores differ in meaning per method. text → keywords Keyword extraction
    3 Build the vocabulary The AMČR keyword lists and the TEATER thesaurus are harvested and nested into one term list, each term pointing back to the records it came from. This is done once, not per run: each release carries the built vocabulary as a CC0 asset, atrium-vocabulary-<version>.zip, with its .sha256. → JSON, CSV, SKOS Collecting
    4 Controlled keywords A language model reads each line among its neighbours on the page and answers with the term that describes it (with the AMČR and TEATER records behind it), the Czech keywords found in it with their English translations, and a confidence. Its answer must fit a schema whose allowed categories are the vocabulary's terms. text → JSON Enriching, Content Analysis
    5 Record the result Statistical keywords go into the record's keywords block, each with its method, score and rank; controlled keywords into its enrichment block, one item per line with its page. Entities that are vocabulary terms are linked to AMČR and AAT identifiers. The paradata names the method, the model and the vocabulary sources. → JSON —

    Step 2 is the statistical kind and step 4 the controlled one; step 3 is done when the vocabulary is built, and a run reads its result. The request chooses which kind runs (kind=statistical, controlled or both, the default), and each kind can fail or be skipped on its own and says so. The service and the batch clients reach the model through OpenRouter or a local Ollama; the research run llm_run.py loads it onto its own GPUs.

    Two limits follow from how the controlled kind works (the service README's Known limits). The schema keeps a category inside the vocabulary, not on topic: a model can be forced onto an unrelated term that shares the start of the word it began. And confidence_score is not calibrated: most lines get one of two values, so do not rank or filter on it.

    Provenance and licence¶

    Every run records its paradata, which lists the components that ran, each with its licence, as declared in the tool's para_config.txt. The service returns the paradata in its response, as a Process Run Crate CreateAction; the command-line and batch runs write it as a log (JSON), and the controlled ones name the vocabulary build they used. The statistical methods differ: the legacy method uses only the standard library, KeyBERT is MIT and loads a sentence-transformers model (Apache-2.0), and YAKE is AGPL-3.0; a run that selects YAKE lists it. The vocabularies are CC0. The language model is not in that list: its own licence or its provider's terms apply to the controlled kind, and every controlled run records the model's id. The paradata's computed label, the most restrictive of the licences listed, is not the licence of the output: Licences and paradata.

    In the document record the tool is the successor of llm-enrich as a writer: the contract lets it write the enrichment and forms blocks and entities[].pid, and, since v1.2.0-beta, the keywords block (atrium-project#73). It writes keywords when the statistical kind ran — the keywords of the document and of each page, every one with its method, score and rank — and enrichment with entities[].pid when the controlled kind contributed; it writes no forms. The two kinds stay in their own blocks and never share a list, and a re-run replaces each block whole. Records already written keep the program id llm-enrich; the contract accepts both names for the one writer.

    Where it sits¶

    • After nlp-enrich in the scanned-document pipeline — Pipelines → W1 — and after digital-convert in the born-digital route (Pipelines → W2). It reads the record, so it does not care which of the two made it.
    • Before the optional TEITOK projection. nlp-enrich's /project_record reads the keywords and writes them into the TEITOK header, so the chain is nlp-enrich → keyword-extract → nlp-enrich's projection.
    • The source of the translator's vocabulary — Pipelines → W7. The vocabulary asset of each release can be read by the translator instead of a harvest of its own.
    • Vocabulary keywords step of the AMČR text workflow 0xSpVP.

    On other platforms¶

    SSH Open Marketplace. Tool record j9fqxo, "Keyword extraction from archival documents (statistical and vocabulary-controlled)". It described the llm-enrich stage until 24 October 2026, when it was re-pointed to this repository.

    Galaxy. Inputs map to json (the record) and txt; outputs to json. Inside ATRIUM, DARIAH's Galaxy tools already chain PDF text extraction with vocabulary-driven information extraction (task 4.2.1). The container is the service image ghcr.io/ufal/atrium-keyword-extract-api:<version> (POST /extract_keywords); for Galaxy the statistical kind is the capability to wrap. The controlled kind needs a language-model service, and its output is still research output.
