**The abstract** (the manuscript of 25 October, 201 words, citations left out):

> Digitisation preserves fragile archaeological records and broadens access, but their contents remain difficult to
> search and reuse without further processing. Historical archives combine typewritten and handwritten notes, forms,
> tables, drawings, photographs, and born-digital files, each requiring different treatment. This paper presents a
> modular text-processing workflow developed within ATRIUM with the Archaeological Information System of the Czech
> Republic (AIS CR). Six specialised services can classify scanned pages, extract text and layout from born-digital
> files, assess OCR-line quality, and add linguistic annotation, candidate keywords, controlled-vocabulary
> suggestions, and English translation. Each service enriches one shared JSON record per document, which keeps
> source links, spatial information, and processing provenance together. The record keeps component licences and
> service terms apart from permission to process a source and from the rights applicable to an output. We report
> implementation and contract-verification evidence, including smoke checks for scanned and born-digital routes.
> Five vision-only page classifiers were evaluated on their respective fold-specific, page-level test partitions of
> the public CC BY-NC 4.0 dataset derivative deposited in LINDAT/CLARIAH-CZ: Top-1 accuracy ranges from 94.64% to
> 95.87%, and from 93.87% to 95.60% on pages whose documents are absent from the corresponding training fold. We
> discuss the workflow's archaeological value, limitations, and planned operational pilot.
