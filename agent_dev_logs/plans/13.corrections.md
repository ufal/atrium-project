## Items still to enter _(11-11; superseded 11-12 by R1–R16, mapped above)_

Words: + and − are rough counts of words added and removed; the paper is near the 5,000-word limit, so the set is
kept about neutral.

**1. The record keeps run identifiers, not stamps (C4; D1).** l.330:

> Each service stamps the blocks it writes with a reference to the paradata of its run. It includes the software and
> model versions, container image, configuration, timing, and the licences or terms of the components used

Proposed:

> The record keeps, for each block, only the identifier of the run that wrote it. Each run is described in its own
> paradata: the software and model versions, container image, parameters, timing, and the licences or terms of the
> components used

(+6.) Figure 2: the brace label "each block's stamp: `program`, `run_id`, `paradata_ref`" becomes "each block: the
`run_uuid` of the run that wrote it"; the arrow label `regenerable` becomes "derived" (the field leaves the record);
the caption's "and stamps them with a reference to the paradata of its run" becomes "and the record keeps the
identifier of the run that wrote each block".

**2. Licences: three layers, nothing computed (D2; contract §5.7).** l.330, from "The rights applicable to an
output…" to the end of the paragraph:

> The rights applicable to an output cannot be automatically asserted, as licence inheritance and compatibility
> cannot be established programmatically. This must be decided by an authorised party passing the data to the
> workflow. Therefore, outputs of the pipeline state that no licence is asserted, and the tools, models, datasets,
> and services used are stored as processing provenance, including their original licence.

Proposed:

> No licence is computed from the components. The licence of the source document, or the archive's default, is
> recorded in the RO-Crate of the archive's record, and derived outputs inherit it; the paradata of each processing
> step list the tools, models, datasets, and services used, each with its own licence or terms.

(−18.) Figure 2: the crate box "record and paradata; … root licence: none asserted" becomes "the archived record,
built by AMCR; … licence of the source, inherited by outputs"; rights box 3 "Rights in an output: asserted by an
authorised party" becomes "Rights in the content: the source's licence, in the crate".

**3. digital-convert reads every file first and calls no other service (K4TEL; H11).** §3.2, l.315 (the whole
paragraph), currently: page classification → ATR or existing ALTO/PAGE → digital-convert for born-digital files →
"flagged for fallback ATR" → ocr-postprocess "only adds reading-order and line-quality information". Proposed:

> Each incoming file is first read by \texttt{atrium-digital-convert}, which assesses the text layer of every page,
> extracts the text it can with lightweight, format-specific readers, and describes visual elements, without calling
> other services. From these signals, the workflow sends pages with missing or unusable text to ATR, so native text
> is reused where it is reliable and ATR is run only where it is needed. ATR runs outside the six services, together
> with page classification, which indicates the visible content of each page and preserves the category scores for
> later analysis. Existing ALTO or PAGE XML can also be post-processed directly. \texttt{atrium-ocr-postprocess}
> then orders the lines of the recognised pages and labels the quality of every line, whether it comes from ATR or
> from a digital file.

(+25.) If images and tabular files are not yet read by digital-convert when the paper is submitted, "Each incoming
file" becomes "Each PDF or office file" (the readers for images and CSV are planned:
[digital-convert's slice](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-digital-convert/blob/test/agent_dev_logs/plans/doc-schema-v2.plan.md)).

**4. Handwriting is recognised by the ATR step (K4TEL; contract §7 q. 8).** Figure 1 caption:

> ATR is an external step, to which born-digital pages without usable text can also be sent; handwritten pages,
> identified by their page category, are left to a separate recognition tool.

Proposed:

> ATR is an external step, run with page classification, which recognises print and readable handwriting; born-digital
> pages without usable text are sent to it as well.

(−4.) And §5, l.507, the end of the bullet "…or for a separate handwriting-recognition system." becomes "…or for
re-recognition with handwriting recognition." In §3.2 (after item 3's ATR sentence), one sentence if the words
allow: "Handwritten identifiers, technical notes, and signatures are usually misrecognised." (+8.)

**5. The order of the steps (K4TEL).** Figure 1 is redrawn (spec below); its caption's first sentence becomes
