## IJDL manuscript — post-submission review and corrections for the next editorial stage

@K4TEL, I have reviewed the submitted manuscript **IJDL-D-26-00077** in the context of the published classifier, annotated datasets, Kate's thesis and the IJDL submission requirements.

**My overall assessment is positive.** Compared with the broader ATRIUM proceedings manuscript, this is a coherent standalone research paper with a clear contribution, substantial experimental work and a suitable connection to Kate's thesis. I do not think we need to restructure it or extend the research significantly.

However, there are several factual, methodological and editorial points that should be resolved **before the paper is published**.

### 1. Please confirm the actual submission status

First, could you check Editorial Manager and report:

- The current status of **IJDL-D-26-00077**, including any editor assignment or review decisions.
- Whether any reviewer comments or revision requests have been received, and the applicable deadlines.
- Which version is currently submitted and whether it differs from the attached PDF or the Overleaf manuscript.
- What changes are currently possible through the journal's submission system.

Please share any editorial correspondence with the co-authors. This is especially important because we need to distinguish corrections suitable for a revised manuscript from the limited corrections possible during production proofs.

Please do not submit another version, change publication metadata or correspond with the editor on substantive matters without first coordinating the response with the co-authors.

### 2. Methodological clarifications — highest scientific priority

**Train/test split and evaluation design (Sections 4.2.3, 4.2.5 and 5.2).**

Please reconcile the descriptions of five-fold cross-validation, the 80/10/10 training/development/test partitions, and the reported fixed 5,449-page test set excluded from training.

The current explanation is not sufficiently clear about how these sets relate. The code uses repeated stratified/periodic sampling; please verify the exact procedure against the stored experiment splits and describe it consistently, including:

- how the five model checkpoints were trained and selected;
- whether the reported result is based on an averaged checkpoint, one selected fold or another procedure;
- which samples contributed to training, model selection and final evaluation;
- whether pages from the same original PDF could occur in both training and test partitions.

The last point matters because page-level classification within previously represented documents is not necessarily equivalent to generalisation to entirely unseen documents. If the data were split by page, say so explicitly and identify that limitation. We do not necessarily need new experiments, but we must correctly represent the evidence.

**Annotation reliability (Section 4.2.1).**

The manuscript states that no formal Cohen's κ was computed, yet gives estimated κ values of approximately 0.5 and 0.9. Please **remove these unmeasured numerical estimates**. Explain the actual collaborative annotation, expert review and adjudication process, including its limitations. A controlled independent agreement study can remain future work.

### 3. Data, licences and reproducibility

Please update the dataset references in line with [atrium-project#21](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-project/issues/21):

- **Original annotated dataset:** [LINDAT 1-5959](http://hdl.handle.net/20.500.12800/1-5959), including the 318 pages with restricted dissemination.
- **Public licence-compatible derivative:** [LINDAT 1-6184](http://hdl.handle.net/20.500.12800/1-6184), excluding those 318 pages.

The paper must make clear which dataset was used for the reported experiments and how the published results can be reproduced or independently checked. Please identify the exact model revisions corresponding to those experiments, and distinguish them from the more recently retrained `v*.4` models.

Also revise statements that describe the dataset, models and code collectively as released under "open-source licences". These are different resources with different legal conditions. The software's MIT licence does not establish the rights to the source page images.

Check that every archival page reproduced in the figures and supplementary PDFs is permitted for publication, with appropriate attribution where required.

### 4. Interpret the model results more carefully

The comparison of CNNs, ViTs, DiT and CLIP is valuable and worth preserving.

However, please clarify that:

- The small differences between the top-performing image-only models are not demonstrated to be statistically significant.
- CLIP's best result comes from a different, single-fold fine-tuning protocol and is therefore not directly equivalent to the five-fold image-only results.
- Inter-model agreement over the 649,508-page collection measures **consistency, not ground-truth accuracy**.
- CLIP's low agreement with other models supports caution when choosing a model for archival use, but does not independently prove overfitting or establish its true error rate on the unannotated collection.

I would also temper "near-perfect", "most efficient" and similar claims where they imply demonstrated performance beyond the tested conditions. The proposed operational preference for RegNetY-16GF is reasonable, but its justification should accurately identify the available evidence.

Likewise, estimates about processing hundreds of thousands or millions of pages should be supported by a reproducible hardware/runtime measurement or presented clearly as estimates.

### 5. Authors, AI declaration and funding

Please correct the official institute name to **Institute of Archaeology of the Czech Academy of Sciences, Prague**, and check all affiliations and author metadata.

In Section 4.2.1, Dana Křivánková is called the fourth author although she is second in the submitted author list. Please correct this and reconcile the order in the journal submission with the arXiv version.

**The AI-use disclosure needs particular attention.** The submitted text says that all generated content was verified and revised by the authors and that all authors read and approved the manuscript. We cannot make such collective statements unless all authors have actually had the opportunity to review the relevant version and explicitly approve it.

Please remove the redundant disclosure from the Introduction and retain one accurate, transparent declaration in the appropriate dedicated section, consistent with Springer Nature's requirements. Describe the actual use of generative tools and the verification process without attributing work or approval to co-authors who have not confirmed it.

Please circulate the final proposed declarations, author contributions and manuscript version for explicit co-author agreement before any resubmission or publication approval.

The funding statement should also acknowledge **AIS CR (LM2023031)** where appropriate, alongside ATRIUM and the acknowledged computing infrastructure.

### 6. Smaller but useful corrections

- **Section 3:** clarify that removing original PDFs after rasterisation refers to temporary processing copies, if that is the case. The text should not imply that the archival source documents themselves were deleted.
- **Figures:** improve the readability of the dense confusion matrices and model-architecture diagrams, especially Figures 9–12 and 17–19. Avoid tiny labels and decorative elements; preserve the results, but make the material readable at published column width.
- **References:** verify repository versions, thesis citation, dataset handles, model identifiers and supplementary-material references.
- **Technical descriptions:** distinguish what was tested in the original study from the current production configuration, which has evolved since the manuscript was prepared.
- **Final language:** perform one focused editorial pass for clarity, consistency and unnecessary repetition, without changing the paper's scientific structure.

### 7. How I suggest we proceed

**Now:** Please confirm the Editorial Manager status and clarify the two main scientific issues: the evaluation splits and the annotation-reliability statement. These are the items for which we most need an authoritative explanation.

**At the next revision opportunity:** Incorporate the factual, methodological and data-availability corrections, along with the declaration and authorship updates. Prepare one consolidated revision and, if requested by the journal, one response-to-reviewers document.

**If the manuscript is accepted without a normal revision round:** Please notify us before approving the final version. We should distinguish routine proof corrections from substantive issues requiring the handling editor's permission; methodological or authorship changes cannot simply be slipped into proofs.

**Before final publication:** All co-authors should receive and explicitly approve the proposed final text, including the author contributions, AI statement, dataset availability and funding declarations.

I do not propose reopening the study, replacing the existing results or starting new experiments unless the editors or a genuine methodological problem make that necessary.

**The objective is to preserve the substantial work already done and ensure that the published scientific record is precise, reproducible and properly approved by its authors.**

A correction to my last comment: the 5,449 evaluation pages were selected to be held out, as the pages that no fold used for training (`result/stats/unused.sh` in page-classification). The script compared file names as text and missed two kinds of training pages:
- zero-padded page numbers: `CTX192100040-01.png` was not matched to page 1 (3,679 pages);
- ids with a letter: `CTX193401761A-11.png` did not match its pattern (617 pages).

These are the 4,296. With names matched correctly, only 130 of the pages are in no fold's training partition. The script is fixed in page-classification [v1.9.6-beta](https://github.com/ufal/atrium-page-classification/releases/tag/v1.9.6-beta), and its README now says what the old figures were measured on.
