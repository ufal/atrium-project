## CAA 2026 proceedings — focused manuscript revision, responsibilities and submission status

@K4TEL and @david-spacil — after reviewing the paper against the original CAA presentation, the current ATRIUM/AMČR work, the CAA submission requirements and comparable proceedings papers, I believe it needs a **substantial but focused revision before submission**.

The material is valuable, but the present text reads too much like a compressed technical description of six repositories. It needs a clearer archaeological motivation, a coherent research argument, and more careful distinctions between demonstrated results, implemented capabilities and future integration.

The deadline is **31 October 2026**. We should concentrate on making the best paper possible from the material and results we already have, without starting new experiments or expanding the scope.

I have discussed this with **@david-spacil**, who has agreed to lead the archaeological part and help bring the manuscript to completion. Please work together on one coherent revision:

- **@K4TEL:** technical and methodological content, existing evaluation results, workflow description, figures, references, final manuscript assembly and submission preparation.
- **@david-spacil:** archaeological background, archival material and its research value, practical examples, interpretation of results, discussion and the overall archaeological argument.

**David Spáčil should be second author**, immediately after Kate, reflecting his substantial contribution to the revised paper.

### 1. Focus the article around one coherent contribution

I would retain the general theme of our CAA presentation, **“Digitized Yet Hidden”**, and the **integrated ATRIUM processing workflow**. We should not reduce the article to page classification alone—that work already has a [separate research preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07558)—but neither should we describe six tools as if each were a separate paper.

The central argument should be:

**Digitisation preserves archaeological archival material, but does not automatically make its content accessible, searchable or reusable. ATRIUM develops a modular, content-aware approach that connects document classification, extraction, text-quality assessment and semantic enrichment to help address this gap.**

The article should explain:

1. **The archaeological problem:** the significance and heterogeneity of archival material and why existing digitisation is insufficient for meaningful use.
2. **Our approach:** combining specialised tools in a coherent workflow for scanned and born-digital materials, rather than relying on one uniform processing method.
3. **What has actually been achieved:** implemented components, established interfaces, existing demonstrations and component-level evaluations.
4. **The wider significance:** how the approach can support archaeological archives and research, what remains difficult, and what the next integration steps are.

The paper should offer a **reasoned methodological contribution with demonstrated components**, not claim to report a completely deployed and evaluated production system.

A possible title is:

**Digitized Yet Hidden: A Modular Processing Workflow for Archaeological Archives**

We can agree on the final title once the revised narrative is ready.

### 2. Suggested structure and division of work

Aim for **approximately 3,500–4,200 words**, comfortably within the CAA limit of 5,000 words excluding references.

| Section | Lead | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Introduction and archaeological background** | **@david-spacil** | Why archaeological archives matter, what the Prague and Brno collections contain, the limitations of digitisation alone, and the research problem addressed. Introduce the wider AIS CR/AMČR context. |
| **2. Material and processing challenges** | **@david-spacil + @K4TEL** | Heterogeneous source material, scanned and born-digital documents, page types, OCR/HTR difficulties, text quality, terminology, multilingual content and the need to preserve source context. |
| **3. ATRIUM workflow and methodological approach** | **@K4TEL** | A connected explanation of the processing architecture and six tools, including alternative entry routes, content classification, extraction, quality assessment, NLP, keyword enrichment and translation. Explain their relationships and why these steps are useful. |
| **4. Implementation and available evidence** | **@K4TEL, supported by @david-spacil** | What is implemented and tested, with the existing page-classification evaluation as the main quantitative example. Include an already available cross-tool example if it is sufficiently reliable and easy to present. |
| **5. Discussion and limitations** | **@david-spacil + @K4TEL** | Archaeological usefulness, transferability, limitations of automated processing, uneven quality, data rights and the remaining integration work. Clearly distinguish demonstrated outcomes from expected future benefits. |
| **6. Conclusion** | **Joint** | A concise synthesis of the contribution and its implications for archaeological archival research. |

The six services should remain visible, but introduce them through a **short table of roles, inputs and outputs**, not six long technical subsections.

The **common document representation and provenance support** deserve a short explanation as part of the methodological design. However, the full RO-Crate model, processing-rights questions, detailed Temporal orchestration, Galaxy deployment, advanced semantic applications and complete pipeline evaluation are better developed in **future papers**. We should acknowledge them without allowing them to dominate this article.

### 3. Use existing evidence, and be precise about its meaning

The annotated page-image dataset and classification evaluation are strong existing results. The reported RegNetY-16GF accuracy of **99.16% on the stated evaluation set of 5,449 pages** is worth retaining, with a clear description of its scope and limitations.

However, page-classification accuracy does **not** validate the accuracy or usefulness of all downstream components.

Likewise, we should distinguish between:

- Tools that are **implemented and tested independently**;
- Processing steps demonstrated through **existing integration fixtures or tests**;
- The **planned operational integration** of those tools into AMČR.

The full AMČR processing pilot is planned for December 2026–January 2027 and should not be presented as already running in production. Galaxy integration is also not yet a completed published workflow.

Avoid describing processing as categorically *entirely on-premises*: several existing options rely on LINDAT or external LLM services. Source rights and processing policies influence which services can be used.

Similarly, terms such as *FAIR-compliant*, *calibrated*, *seamless*, *state-of-the-art* and *high-throughput* should only be used where the specific claim is actually supported.

**Do not start new evaluations just for this paper.** Where we do not have evidence, we should explain the limitation rather than invent a result.

### 4. Improve the figures, language and references

Please **replace the existing Figure 1** with one clear, readable conceptual diagram explaining the whole workflow:

archival documents → identification of content and processing needs → extraction and quality assessment → linguistic/semantic enrichment → structured information potentially reusable by archaeological systems.

Show the scanned and born-digital paths and distinguish OCR/HTR itself from OCR post-processing. Avoid internal API objects, dozens of model names, GitHub logos and other details that make the figure difficult to read.

Retain **one concise results table** comparing the main page-classification models. Include a representative archaeological source and derived output only if an existing approved, rights-cleared example is readily available.

The manuscript must read as a scientific narrative for **archaeologists and digital-heritage researchers**, not as developer documentation. Use straightforward English, meaningful paragraphs, clear explanations and restrained technical vocabulary.

Please strengthen the references selectively: archaeological archives and digital reuse, FAIR principles, document analysis, relevant standards and the actual classification research. Correct inaccurate technical citations, including the present confusion between LayoutReader and LayoutLM.

Useful models for structure and presentation include:

- [Bridging Interdisciplinary Research Data Management and Data Science through Modular Research Processes](https://doi.org/10.24072/pcjournal.755)
- [Crossing software boundaries: From iDig and ODK to QField](https://doi.org/10.24072/pcjournal.712)

These are examples of presenting technical developments through clearly explained research problems, not templates to copy mechanically.
