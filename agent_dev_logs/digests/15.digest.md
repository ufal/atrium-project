## 🧪 Editorial/scientific review checklist to carry into the next submission

The 10-20 review is positive about the paper's core contribution and argues for a focused correction pass, not new experiments or a wholesale rewrite. Apply the items below against the final manuscript and the chosen venue's rules:

1. **Evaluation protocol:** reconcile five-fold cross-validation, 80/10/10 partitions and the fixed 5,449-page held-out set. Verify the stored splits and explain checkpoint selection and whether pages from the same PDF may cross partitions. State document-level generalisation limitations if the split was by page.
2. **Annotation reliability:** remove estimated Cohen's κ values if none was measured. Describe the actual annotation, expert-review and adjudication procedure and its limitations.
3. **Dataset, model and rights:** distinguish `1-5959` from `1-6184`; identify the dataset used for the results, exact historical model revisions and newer `v*.4` models; verify image/supplementary publication permissions and attribution. Don't label code, weights and images collectively “open source” without separate licensing support.
4. **Interpretation:** small differences among top image-only models are not proven statistically significant; CLIP used a different single-fold protocol; agreement over the 649,508-page collection indicates consistency, not ground-truth accuracy. Temper “near-perfect”, “most efficient” and runtime claims unless backed by evidence.
5. **Authors and declarations:** use the official affiliation *Institute of Archaeology of the Czech Academy of Sciences, Prague*; reconcile author order across journal and arXiv versions; fix the incorrect description of Dana Křivánková's position. Remove redundant AI disclosure and state actual use accurately. Never claim all authors verified/approved text before each has reviewed and explicitly approved it.
6. **Funding and smaller corrections:** include AIS CR (LM2023031) where appropriate; clarify that any removal of PDFs refers to temporary processing copies, not archival originals; improve dense figures (especially 9–12, 17–19); verify references, thesis citation, model/repository versions and supplementary files; perform one focused editorial pass.

No new model experiments are proposed unless the verified protocol reveals an actual scientific problem or the new journal requests them.
