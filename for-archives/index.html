    What the tools do with your documents¶

    This page is for archive and collection staff. It explains, without code, what the ATRIUM tools do with the documents you give them, what you get back, how far to trust it, and what leaves your institution. Unfamiliar words are explained in the glossary.

    Two routes into the chain¶

    Scanned pages. A scan or photograph of a paper page goes this way:

    1. page-classification looks at the image and says what kind of page it is: one of eleven types, such as typed text, a handwritten table, a drawing or a photograph. The type tells a person, or a script, which treatment the page needs next.
    2. OCR turns the text pages into text. OCR is run outside these tools.
    3. ocr-postprocess checks every line of the OCR output and labels its quality. You see which lines can be used as they are, which need correcting, and which need another OCR pass.
    4. nlp-enrich adds grammar and names: each word's base form and part of speech, and the names of people, places and organisations.
    5. keyword-extract adds keywords to the document and its pages.

    Born-digital files. A PDF exported from Word, or an office file, already holds its text. digital-convert reads it directly, with no OCR. Pages without usable text, such as scanned plans inside the PDF, are marked for the scanned route. Most of the AMČR reports tried so far mix both kinds of page, so the choice is made page by page.

    Translation. The translator writes an English version of AMČR metadata records and of OCR pages. Every tag of the document stays where it was; only the text changes.

    Everything a document gains is kept in one file per document, the record, together with a log of which tool did what, when, and with which settings (the paradata).

    What you get back, and what a person should check¶

    Stage What you get back What a person should check
    page-classification a page type for every page, with a score, and the next likely types pages with a low score; typed text taken for mixed text, and handwritten tables taken for typed ones, are the most common mistakes
    ocr-postprocess every text line with its language and a quality label: Clear, Noisy, Trash (illegible), Non-text or Empty short lines without Czech diacritics labelled Clear, since some are OCR garbage; lines in languages other than Czech, which are scored less reliably
    digital-convert the pages and lines in reading order, with headings and tables; for a PDF, where each line sits on the page tables without ruling lines, which are read column by column; letter-spaced captions and map labels, which come apart; pages marked for OCR
    nlp-enrich base forms, parts of speech, grammar and names, in a TEITOK file that can be searched and shown over the scan it works on Czech only; its name recogniser is a general model, not one trained on archaeological texts
    keyword-extract keywords for each document and page; optionally, terms from AMČR's keyword lists and the TEATER thesaurus the vocabulary-based keywords are experimental and not yet evaluated
    translator the same XML document in English no person has checked the translations yet; where a record must stay valid AMČR, use the default "replace" mode

    Each tool's own README lists its limits in full, under "Limits and maturity".

    How reliable the results are¶

    The tools' tests check that the programs do what their documentation says. They do not measure how often the results are right. That has been measured for two tools only:

    • page-classification was measured on about 4,800 pages per model that the model had never seen. It names the right page type first for 94.6–95.9 % of them, and among its first three for 99.8–99.9 %. Born-digital pages, and documents unlike the archaeological reports it was trained on, were not measured (what was measured).
    • ocr-postprocess was checked against 2,067 lines labelled by hand, and its rule changes were measured over both collections: 113,101 documents and 71.8 million lines from ARÚP and ARÚB. One weakness is counted: about 26,000 lines of OCR garbage read Clear. The labels have not been validated in full (what was measured).
    • digital-convert was tried on 15 AMČR excavation reports, 2,514 pages. What went wrong there was fixed or is listed as a limit; there is no accuracy figure (the AMČR samples).
    • nlp-enrich, the translator, and keyword-extract's vocabulary-based keywords have not been measured on archival documents.

    What leaves your institution¶

    The services run on your own infrastructure. Three of them send document text to another service while they work:

    Tool Sends document text out Downloads when it first starts
    page-classification no its five models, from the Hugging Face Hub
    ocr-postprocess no two of its models, from the Hugging Face Hub
    digital-convert no nothing
    nlp-enrich yes: every line, to LINDAT's UDPipe and NameTag, or to copies of them that you run nothing
    keyword-extract only for vocabulary-based keywords: to the language-model service the deployment names its keyword model, from the Hugging Face Hub
    translator yes: the text to translate, to LINDAT's translation service by default (and, with a vocabulary, to LINDAT's UDPipe), or to a model server that you run nothing
    • A download sends no document data. A deployment without internet access can supply the models in advance; each service's runbook says how.
    • LINDAT is a public research service run by ÚFAL, Charles University, under its terms of use.
    • Planned: each document will carry its own permission, and a document without one will not be sent to an external service. AMČR agreed to this on 22 October 2026; the tools do not enforce it yet (Licences and paradata).

    Who holds the rights in the results¶

    The tools record every component they used, each with its own licence or terms of use. They do not decide the rights in what they produce. Those depend first on the source document, which no tool knows. AMČR states the rights of the outputs it publishes. The full explanation is on Licences and paradata.

    Whom to ask¶

    • About a tool, or a result that looks wrong: that tool's issue tracker, linked from the S2 pilot handover.
    • About the pilot: the contacts listed on the S2 pilot handover.
